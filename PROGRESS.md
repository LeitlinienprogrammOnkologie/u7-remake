## How to run

Open the **`godot/`** project in Godot 4.7 (C# / .NET 8). Data root is the repo (`u7/STATIC/U7MAP` must exist). `u7/`, `exult/` and `assets/` are not tracked; [README.md](README.md) explains how to provide them. A new game starts from `STATIC/INITGAME.DAT` (not `u7/GAMEDAT`, which holds a game already past the opening): Trinsic **1079, 2214**, hour **6:00**, with the opening scene.

| Key / input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD / arrows | Walk while held (fast; Shift: medium) |
| Right button | Exult's: hold to walk toward the cursor (speed by distance), let go to stop; a double right-click walks a path there; a right-click on a gump closes it |
| Left button | Hold on open ground to walk toward the cursor, a quick click there walks a path (the user's pick, besides the right button); drag items; a click on a thing names it (half a second later, unless it is a double-click) |
| Shift+click | Teleport avatar to tile |
| Double-click / E | Use object or run NPC usecode `0x400+id` |
| I | Avatar inventory |
| C | Toggle combat (walk to nearest foe and auto-strike) |
| F4 | Toggle avatar invincibility (hits still flash / bark) |
| [ ] | Skip −1 / +1 hour |
| Home | Return to Trinsic start |
| PgUp / PgDn | Debug: avatar lift +1 / -1 in place (re-checks eggs at the new lift) |
| F5 / F9 | Save / load the quick slot (`saves/bg/quick/`, the mode's folder) |
| Esc | Close the open gumps; with none, the save/load screen (as the paperdoll's disk) |
| F6 | Debug: lethal damage to the avatar with global flag 0x57 set, so usecode 0x60E puts the party in the Fellowship shelter in Paws (836,1733); Shift+F6 leaves the flag alone, in which case the original restarts the game |
| M | Toggle music |
| F2 | Usecode / gump debug |
| F3 | Combat arena (heal, spawn 3 rats, combat on) |
| Mouse wheel | Zoom 1–8× |

### Agent console (automated play-testing)

With the environment variable `U7_AGENT=<dir>` set, `Game/U7Game.Agent.cs` reads one command per line from `<dir>/cmd.txt` and appends results to `<dir>/out.txt`, each ending in `DONE <n>`. Game time is frozen between commands, music is off, and conversation text (`SAY [face<slot> <face shape>]` when a face speaks), barks, fades (`FADE in|out, n steps (ms)`) and cursor flashes (`FLASH too heavy`) are logged; a command ends once a fade or flash it started is over. Commands: `look [r]` (actors with their alignment and facing, things not okay to take marked `owned`), `find <text>` (`find owned`: the things not okay to take, in containers too), `npc <num|name>` (also its attack mode, the player's or usecode's, and oppressor), `create <shape> [frame] [qual]` (a test shortcut: a new thing in the avatar's hands or pack, okay to take, as usecode's `create_new_object` + `give_last_created`), `call <fun> [id|npc:n] [event]` (a test shortcut: runs a usecode function, hex, on an object, by default the avatar with event 1; the spells' functions this way skip the book), `state`, `inv [npc|id]`, `flags` (the global flags changed since the start) or `flags <hex> <0|1>` (sets one, a test shortcut), `setflag <npc|id> <flag> [0|1]` (an object flag by number or `ObjFlag` name, e.g. `setflag npc:1 invisible`, set as usecode sets it: an actor's timer starts), `timer [n] [hours-ago]`, `stubs`, `tile <x> <y> [z]`, `eggs [type] [radius]` (the eggs near the avatar, of one type by number or name: criteria, area, chance, data 1 and 2, hatched; default 40 tiles), `walk <x> <y>`, `walkto <id|npc:num>`, `steer <dir|0-7> <sec> [ms]`, `tp <x> <y> [z]`, `talk`/`use <id|npc>` (a double-click: the avatar opens its paperdoll, an NPC runs its usecode, an enemy is attacked in combat mode; refused while usecode runs, as is `save`), `drop <id> <x> <y> [z] [count|ask]` (a drag onto a tile: the whole stack, or part of it by count or through the "how many?" dialog; placed, refused, or taken by whom it was dropped on), `quantity <n>|cancel` (answers the dialog), `spells <book> <n>...` (spells added to a spellbook, the first bookmarked; a test shortcut), `key <name>` (a key press through the game's key handling, by Godot's key names: `pageup`, `home`, `left`, `enter`; says whether the spellbook has the keys), `take <id>` (a drag into the avatar's gump, a theft if the thing isn't okay to take; from the world with the mouse's checks: what weighs nothing or is out of reach is refused, as `put`'s), `sail <x> <y>` (in barge mode: the barge's centre heads there, as with the button held, until it stops), `put <id> <container-id>` (a drag into a container, also off a ready spot, or onto an actor's paperdoll, readied in its best free spot; a theft as `take`), `attack <id>` (a double-click in combat mode: anyone, not only enemies), `name <id>|gump` (a left click on a thing or the topmost gump, Exult `show_items`: the name shown over it), `rmouse <x> <y> <sec>|double` (the right button held over a tile, or double-clicked), `rclose` (a right-click on the topmost gump), `drag <id>` (the start of a mouse drag of a thing in the world, with Exult's checks and their flash, then put back), `cursor <x> <y>` (the cursor with the mouse over a tile, and the walking speed it means; headless games have no mouse), `endgame [won|lost]` (a test shortcut: usecode's `run_endgame`; its parts are logged as `ENDGAME ...` lines, `cont` skips as a key does, and the game stays when it is over), `book [page]` (the open spellbook's page, spells and bookmark; turns to a page) and `cast <spell>` (a double-click on a spell in it), `close`, `cont [n|all]` (also closes a picture or the Wizard Eye, as Esc: the WAIT line names the sprite, the map's mark, where a view elsewhere looks and the eye's time left), `eye <dir> [tiles]` (the Wizard Eye moved as the held right button does, a tile a step, 0 north clockwise), `choose <answer|#n>`, `num <n>`, `click <id>` or `click <x> <y> [z]` (a tile, for `click_on_item`), `wait <sec>`, `hour <h> [m]` (also prints `light`), `sprite <n> [frame]` (a SPRITES.VGA animation over the avatar, from a frame), `damage <n> [type] [id|npc:n]` (damage through the normal path, to the avatar or, a test shortcut, any actor: tells whether it flashed red or showed the outline, and a death), `die [restart]` (F6: a lethal hit on the avatar; flag 0x57 is set so the party wakes in Paws, unless `restart`), `weather [<n> [min] | lightning | eggs [radius]]` (the weather, the clock's overcast and fog counts, the flashes so far and each weather effect; sets the weather as usecode does, 15 minutes unless given; adds usecode's lightning; lists the weather eggs near the avatar with their weather and minutes), `light` (the palette blend, dungeon, final palette, the light spell's minutes left and strength, Exult's light level and class, the avatar's carried light, and the top lights: shape, frame, brightness, tile, kind, radius), `save`/`load <slot>` (in the mode's saves folder), `game [bg|si|saga]` (the game and mode, or a new game in another), `esc` (the Esc key: gumps closed, else the save/load screen) and `saveload [choose <row>|name <text>|save|load|delete|cancel|quit|yes|no]` (the save/load screen, opened as the disk opens it; prints its rows, choice, buttons and details), `menu [journey|new|intro|credits|quotes|endgame|quit|up|down|key|esc]` (the main menu and the intro, which headless games start without; `cont` skips an intro's scene, `cont all` the intro), `speech <n>|intro|endgame <7-9>|play <n>|state` (a voice decoded, or played muted), `prop <npc|id> <name|n> <value>` (an actor's property, a test shortcut), `shot <name>` (windowed only), `quit` (how a windowed run ends: only headless games may be stopped from outside). Object ids come from `look`/`find` output. Helpers: `pwsh scripts/agent/restart.ps1 [-Load <slot>] [-Io <dir>]` starts it headless (I/O in `agent_io/`, or its own folder with `-Io`, so several games can run side by side, each restarting only itself), `python scripts/agent/agent.py "<cmd>" ...` sends commands and prints the results (to `$U7_AGENT` when set). `walkto` goes to a free tile beside the target and `walk` says where it stopped short; `find` also searches what the party carries; `npc`/`look` give an actor's hit points and its status (asleep, poisoned, paralyzed, invisible, charmed, cursed, protected, might); `choose` and `num` refuse while nothing asks for them (text waiting for a click needs `cont` first). The Trinsic murder chapter (opening, stables, Finnigan, Spark, the chest, Gilberto, Gargan, the report and map quiz, Johnson's gate) and Britain's first visit were played through this way.

### Temporary objects (current)

- Exult `Game_map::cache_out`: whenever the avatar enters a new superchunk, spawned monsters, their corpses and items flagged temporary outside the surrounding 3×3 superchunks are deleted (`GameMap.CacheOut`). Auto-reset monster eggs therefore respawn on a later visit, as in Exult.

### Shape data (current)

- `Data/ShapeFrame` ports Exult's `Shape_frame`: a frame as palette indices, its RLE unpacked into scans (runs of opaque pixels placed from the hotspot) over one packed index array. Raw 8×8 terrain frames (shapes 0-149, length/64 frames) are eight scans at -8,-8 with extents 8, 8, -1, -1, where Exult's `copy8` paints them. `Reflect` is Exult's: transposed into a square of 255s and scanned again, so index 255 drops out (SHAPES.VGA has no opaque 255). `Covers` is `has_point`: a pixel of slack at each end of a scan; flats get Exult's extents test, which misses their last row and column.
- `VgaShapeFile.DecodeFrame(shape, frame, wrap)` is Exult's `Shape::read`. Without wrapping (SHAPES.VGA, SPRITES.VGA) a frame past the last is none, bit 5 asks for the reflection, and terrain ignores the bits above 31. GUMPS, FONTS and FACES keep wrapping frame numbers and decode through it unchanged (checked frame by frame against the old decoder).
- `Data/U7Palette`: PALETTES.FLX entries as stored (`Raw6`, 6-bit), converted with Exult's `Get_color8` (v·255/63, as the extracted PNGs were; before, v·4) and `FindColor` (Exult `find_color`, below the rotating colours by default). Gumps, fonts, faces and sprites got up to 3 levels brighter with it.
- `Data/XformTables`: XFORM.TBL's 17 tables, stored in reverse as Exult does: table i serves index 0xEE + i, and file entry 0, the invisible actors' table, is the last. Without the file they are built from Exult's blends (`create_trans_table`), which disagree with the file on 139-252 of each table's 256 entries.
- Verified with temporary startup code: all 14171 SHAPES.VGA frames decoded with the day palette equal the extracted PNGs pixel for pixel; their extents equal `ShapeCatalog`'s (except terrain, which the CSV gives as 0, 0, 7, 7); every reflection's extents equal `GetFrame(frame | 32)`'s and its pixels are the transposed frame's; 183 shapes use indices 0xE0-0xFE, the same shapes as the cycle scan (its other 58 entries are animated shapes without them). Decoding every frame takes about 40 ms, reflecting them all 130 ms more.

### Rendering (current)

- Animated shapes are Exult's `Frame_animator`s (`World/Animators`): every TFA animation type (time-synched in step with their own phase, looping with lingering on frame 0, hourly, random, non-looping), on Exult's 100 ms beat, only while on screen; the frame is the object's own, so usecode sees it. Chunks are painted along Exult's diagonals. Gumps and their buttons are hit on their pixels, not their boxes.
- Roofs: the lift above which nothing is drawn is Exult's `is_roof` on the chunk cache (actors count, as in Exult), worked out when the avatar moves (`GameMap.RoofOverAvatar`, Exult `skip_above_actor`), not every frame by scanning objects.
- `WorldView` paints the world as Exult does, into an 8-bit buffer of palette indices (`Rendering/IndexBuffer8`, Exult's `Image_buffer8`) the size of the view plus a pixel round it, with a parallel glow plane: six bits of glow (magic pixels, see "Lighting (current)") and two marks for the world shader, roof (building-class objects from lift 3, which window light skips) and pane (the see-through pixels of glass), plus a pane plane noting each pane pixel's window and table. Each frame: index 0 everywhere, the chunks' flats, the flat RLE objects, the other objects in dependency order, the dungeon blackness (index 0, Exult `paint_blackness`), then the sprites. Painters clip per scan.
- Flats are Exult's `Chunk_terrain::render_flats`: a 128×128 picture of indices per terrain (raw 8×8 terrain frames only), kept for the 256 most recently used terrains.
- The buffer goes up as two R8 textures, the index plane drawn once and the glow plane beside it (`glow_tex`), straight from the buffer's arrays, through the world shader, which looks the index up in `Rendering/WorldPalette`'s two rows and lights it (see "Lighting (current)"). Usecode's fades are drawn over everything by `Rendering/ScreenFx` (see "Lighting (current)").
- Colour cycling is Exult's `Game_window::rotatecolours`: every 100 ms of real time (the user's pick between Exult's 100 and 200) each of the ranges 0xFC-0xFE, 0xF8-0xFB, 0xF4-0xF7, 0xF0-0xF3, 0xE8-0xEF and 0xE0-0xE7 moves round by one, the last colour into the first slot (`Image_window8::rotate_colors`), and the palette texture is updated only then. It keeps going in gump mode, conversations and books, as in Exult, but holds while usecode waits for a target click (`click_on_item`), as Exult's other `Get_click` waits do. Sprites' translucent pixels have already been turned into other indices by the tables, so only opaque colours cycle, as in Exult.
- Verified with pairs of windowed shots about 330 ms apart, object animation held still: at the sea off Trinsic (its sparkles), a campfire (3824 screen pixels changed) and elsewhere, every changed pixel lies on a buffer index in 0xE0-0xFE.

### Renderer command stream (current)

R1 of [docs/RENDERER.md](docs/RENDERER.md), 2026-10-13. No visible change.

- `WorldView.PaintView` no longer paints as it walks: it records what it decides (the flats, the flat objects, the other objects in dependency order, the dungeon blackness, missiles, homing missiles and sprites) as an ordered command stream, `Rendering/PaintStream`, and the backend replays it once the frame is recorded. The eight ops are `IndexBuffer8`'s methods (RENDERER.md 5.1): `Copy8` (a terrain's flats), `Fill8` (the blackness), `Rle`, `RleTranslucent`, `RleThroughPanes`, `RleTransformed`, `RleOutline` and `RleSolid`. A command holds the frame at the scale painted and its identity (`ArtRef`: SHAPES, SPRITES or a terrain, the shape and frame), the hotspot in buffer pixels, the glow, haze and window bytes, the table, colour and scale, its pane mask (the windows a hidden effect shows through, kept by the stream rather than in one scratch array) and the object painted. Everything else `DrawObject` did stays in the recording pass: the animators' frames, the paint stamps a click picks by, the barks, the windows' slots.
- The CPU backend is `PaintStream.Replay`: the buffer's methods with the same arguments in the same order as before, so the planes are the reference by construction. The flats are recorded as raw frames (a `ShapeFrame` with its top left at the hotspot) like everything else, and a terrain's flats used by the frame being recorded are never taken over for another terrain (the cache grows for that frame instead): painted at once that was a cache miss, deferred it would have painted the wrong terrain (RENDERER.md, section 10).
- Verified: the suite's 27 golden dumps the same before and after (`render_suite.py check`, two fresh headless runs), `art check` with the original art at 4 finding no differing pixel in `rs_glass` (the teleport sprite over the invisible avatar, Iolo's outline, the window over the ghost over the field), `rs_castle_night`, `rs_destard` and `rs_trinsic`. The cost: `art bench` on `rs_trinsic` at 1280x800 the same as R0's matrix row for row (paint at 1 from 3.7 ms at zoom 2 to 1.7 at zoom 8, at 4 from 23.1 to 4.4, the uploads alike), and on `rs_castle_night` 10.9 ms at zoom 4 with 4x art against R0's 9.6; recording is 1-2.5 ms of the paint (the walk of the chunks and objects, R0's "about 2 ms whatever the buffer"), the replay the rest. The console's `art` line now shows the recording time and the frame's command count (1,500-3,500 commands); right after a load its smoothed times are still high from the first frames (the pack's frames unpacked), so measure with `art bench` or after a few seconds.

### Renderer GPU backend (current)

R2 of [docs/RENDERER.md](docs/RENDERER.md), 2026-10-13: the feasibility gate, passed. The CPU backend stays the default until the user has approved the GPU's picture.

- `Rendering/GpuCompositor` replays the command stream on the GPU (RENDERER.md 5.2's reference resolve): the frames go into one storage buffer as dense texels (index, coverage, the mix bytes), keyed by the command's identity (file, shape, frame) and the scale and emptied when the art changes; each frame the commands are clipped and binned to 16x16 tiles on the CPU (0.2-0.4 ms), uploaded with the tile lists and the pane masks on the render thread, and one compute work group a tile resolves its pixels bottom up, every op as `IndexBuffer8` does it, into four RGBA8 textures (index, glow, mix, panes) that the world shader draws and samples exactly as the CPU's. Nothing above the planes changed; the colour plane (true colour) is not resolved, so with it on the CPU paints.
- Console: `art renderer cpu|gpu|auto` (kept in `saves/settings.cfg`; `auto` is the GPU where a rendering device exists, which is never headless or under Compatibility), `art resolve reference|skip` (R3), `art store <MB>` (R4, the store's budget), `art` says which backend and resolve painted the last frame and why the CPU did if the GPU was wanted (the device starting, the textures being remade at a new size, the art not fitting the store), the binning and submit times, the commands by op and tile entries, and the store's use by page; `art dump` reads the GPU's planes back, so `art diff` compares the backends; `vsync on|off` for `art bench`.
- Verified bit-exact to the CPU (index, glow, pane and mix planes; `art dump` on each backend of the same frame, `art diff`) on `rs_glass` (the window over the ghost over the fire field, the teleport sprite over the invisible avatar through the invisible table, Iolo's poison outline), `rs_castle_night`, `rs_destard` and `rs_trinsic` at zoom 4 with the 4x art; a window resize mid-run (one CPU frame while the textures are remade); the fallback headless; a clean exit (the device's resources freed).
- Measured with `art bench`, 1280x800, vsync off, on this PC's Ryzen 5 7535U with its integrated Radeon, the whole frame in ms, CPU against GPU backend: Trinsic's street at zoom 4 with 4x art 16.2 against 3.8 (the resolve itself 1.8 ms on the GPU), forced 4x at zoom 2 (4.1 million pixels) 49.8 against 8.2, zoom 8 7.5 against 3.3; the throne room at night 17.1 against 3.6 at zoom 4, 47.9 against 7.9 at zoom 2; at 1x 8.6 against 2.5 at zoom 4 (no planes go up each frame). ### Modern colour (current)

R5 of [docs/RENDERER.md](docs/RENDERER.md) (5.5 as built), 2026-10-13. `art modern on|off` (kept in `saves/settings.cfg`), on by default: shown the shots, the user found no discernible difference and took either look (2026-10-13).

- `Rendering/ColourLut`: the ambient and lit palettes as 32³ lookup tables over RGB (`ImageTexture3D`, sampled trilinearly), each cell today's true-colour rule evaluated at its nearest palette entry (`PaletteSlopes.Slope`), rebuilt when the palettes change (`WorldPalette.Lut`, 6 ms; 64³ by `art lut 64`, 47 ms); the seventeen translucency tables' lookup tables as a storage buffer the GPU compositor samples by hand (`lut_turn`) when a true colour is seen through a table, instead of the anchor rule. A soft pixel blended over the ground (R4) thus needs no anchor. `art lut` reports the tables' size, build time and error: on the palettes' own colours the ambient table strays at most 1 level (0.02 on average at 22:00, exact by day), the lit table none, 32³ and 64³ alike, so the seam between an original pixel and a hi-res one of the same colour is that level.
- The world shader in modern mode: own colours through the tables; the ambient and lit colours converted to linear light, the lights, the glow boost, the cloud shade and the mist mixed there (their colours converted too), the result converted back. ### Hi-res art (current)

- Why: at zoom 4 a world pixel covers 4 times the window's content scale on screen (10.8 screen pixels full screen on a 4K monitor), so the original art is blocky. The user asked for more resolution without zooming out, quality over effort, and AI pre-upscaling (2026-10-10). Exult's own answer is its scalers (`imagewin/`: Point, Bilinear, 2xSaI, Scale2x, Hq2-4x, 2-4xBR), run on the whole 8-bit screen each frame; here the art itself is redrawn.
- Offline (`scripts/upscale/`, its own Python venv, see README): `u7art.py` reads the art as the C# readers do; `generate.py` shows each frame of SHAPES, SPRITES, GUMPS and FACES.VGA to a 4x model (`models.py`: Real-ESRGAN x4plus and its anime 6B, 4x-NXbrz, 4x-Rebout-Blend, 4x-Lollypop; converted to ONNX once, run by ONNX Runtime on DirectML) in the day palette, its edges bled outward and mirrored. Terrain tiles go among their most frequent neighbours (counted over U7MAP and U7CHUNKS), pieces built into floors, walls and roofs (opaque along 85% of every edge) among copies of themselves a footprint apart, painted north-west first, so the model doesn't darken what shows by the slab sides their neighbours cover (a seam every 32 pixels on Lord British's upper floor until then). `pack.py` turns the colours back into palette art: the silhouette is the opacity upscaled bicubic and cut at a half (built pieces keep their exact stairs); colour-cycling pixels and a translucent shape's see-through pixels keep their original index, blown up, so Exult's rotation and XFORM tables still apply; every other pixel becomes the nearest of the 224 colours below 0xE0 (CIELAB, through a 64^3 table) plus a second colour and how far towards it. The packs are `assets/hires/<model>/<file>.hrp` (local, gitignored); about ten minutes per model on an RX 9070 XT.
- In the game, `Rendering/HiresArt` gives `WorldView` each frame at the art's scale (the pack's, else the original blown up), reflections made from the hi-res frame; `IndexBuffer8` gains a mix plane (a second index and how far), touched only once a frame has painted a mixed pixel and then uploaded as an RG8 texture beside the others. Painting is Exult's with the positions times the scale; a status outline is Exult's round the original frame, blown up, and with a pack a band a world pixel deep along the inside of the redrawn silhouette (`ShapeFrame.Outline`), as Exult paints it on a frame's edge pixels; hit tests keep the original frames. The world shader mixes the two indices' ambient and lit colours, so every palette, blend, light and fade is Exult's as before. The sharp fill (an option) blends a texel's edges over one screen pixel, so every texel shows the same size at a fractional scale. Below zoom 3 (more than 2.5 million buffer pixels) or where a texel would cover less than 0.6 screen pixels, the original art is painted. Gumps, the items in them, pictures and faces come from the packs as textures that report the original's size, filtered smoothly with mipmaps; fonts stay the original.

### Rendering order (current)

- Mouse picking chooses the object painted last in the most recent frame at the cursor (`PaintStamp`), so clicks agree with what is drawn on top. An object is under the cursor where Exult's `has_point` says so (`ShapeFrame.Covers`): on its frame's opaque pixels, or one pixel past either end of a row of them; items in gumps likewise.
- Paint order is Exult's: each chunk computes paint dependencies between its non-flat objects (and its neighbours') with a port of `Game_object::compare` the first time it is drawn, refreshed whenever an object moves. Flat objects paint first, then each chunk row-major with every object painted after its dependencies (`Game_render::paint_object`). The dependencies are Exult's `Game_object_set`, a `std::set` of pointers, so two that don't depend on each other paint in the order they were made in practice; here `Data/ObjectSet`, sorted by id (2026-10-13). They were hash sets, walked in the order found, which hung on which chunk was painted first: a camera path could swap Iolo and the blood pool on his tile under the avatar. The old single sort key survives only in `FindNearby`'s ordering.
- Sleeping NPCs lie on top of the nearest free bed at bed lift + bed height in the sleep frame (Exult `Sleep_schedule`), unmake the bed on lying down and make it again on getting up.

### Mods (current)

- A mod is Exult's: `--mod <name>` (or `U7_MOD`; `restart.ps1 -Mod <name>`) makes `mods/<name>/data` the patch folder (`U7Paths.PatchDir`, Exult's `<PATCH>`, the folder a mod's `.cfg` names as `__MOD_PATH__/data`).

### Weather (current)

- `World/Weather.cs` ports Exult's weather effects (effects.cc), which `EffectsManager` keeps on its own time queue: each has a first event a delay after it is made and lasts its game minutes from then (25 standard delays, 5 s, a minute); events run in time order at the times they were due, so long frames catch up step by step. `OnRemoved` stands in for Exult's destructors. How it is drawn: see the bullets after the verification.
  - Storm (2) = the storm, overcast clouds for a minute longer, rain for two more minutes starting gradually 20 ms to 1 s in, and lightning for two minutes less, up to 0.5 s after the rain. Snowstorm (1) = clouds and snow the same way. Sparkle (3, Ambrosia and the generators) = 33 sparkles, numbered 3 and from the egg too, shown indoors. Fog (4) = the clock is foggy from its start to its end, plus 100 sparkles 0.25-1.25 s in. Overcast (5) and clouds (6) are `Clouds_effect`s.
  - `Clouds_effect`: 2-6 clouds (one more half the time when overcast), wind dx, dy of -2..2 px per 100 ms (never both 0), some clouds half as fast again; overcast unless it is weather 6.
  - `Rain_effect`: raindrops, snowflakes or sparkles (Exult's `Basicdrop` frames and steps, `DropKind`), 100 ms steps; a gradual one (the storms') adds 0-4 drops a step up to 200 and takes 0-14 a step in its last 2.5 s (none come back then, a fix, see below).
  - `Lightning_effect`: a 25-50 ms flash, then another after 4-7 s (one time in 50 after 40-280 ms), one at a time, until its time is up (no flash starts past it, a fix, see below). A storm's lightning doesn't flash while the avatar is in a dungeon; usecode's always flashes.
- `EffectsManager.SetWeather` is Exult `Egg_object::set_weather`: 0 minutes means 6000; unless the new weather is fog, all weather goes first if it is sparkles or differs from the current one (`get_weather`: the newest numbered effect), so the same weather again is added alongside; 0 clears it. On every chunk the avatar enters, the weather from eggs 120 or more tiles away ends (`Game_window::emulate_cache`). Only the numbered effect carries the egg, so a storm ends there but its clouds, rain and lightning run their time, Exult's quirk, kept.
- `GameClock` keeps Exult's overcast and fog counts (`set_overcast`, `set_fog`): cloudy while the overcast count is above 0, foggy while the fog count is above 0 between 6:00 and 20:59. Weather is not saved (Exult resets it and the counts on a restore).
- Where it differs from Exult (2026-10-08, the user's pick: Exult's bugs, fixed where its code shows what it meant):

  - The fog count stays true and the hours apply when it is read. Exult sets it to 0 when a fog starts or ends at night, so a night fog ended by day leaves -1 and cancels the next one; Exult also counts down a fog removed before it started. Exult shows fog only on the dawn and day palettes, so the one visible change is that a night fog still going at 6:00 shows.
  - Lightning removed mid-flash clears the one-flash-at-a-time flag; Exult leaves it set, which stops all lightning until a restore.
  - Lightning past its end goes without flashing. Exult checks the end only as a flash ends, so storm lightning held back in a dungeon flashed once out of a clear sky when the avatar came out.
  - A gradual rain adds no drops in its last 2.5 s. Exult adds 0-4 again once none are left, so a few flicker until it stops.

### Walking (current)

- Blocking is Exult's `Chunk_cache`: per chunk, tile and lift a count of solid objects with height (`Data/ChunkBlocking`), built per chunk on first use and updated as objects enter, leave or change shape. A wall counts in every chunk its footprint covers (before, walls reaching in from the next chunk east or south were missed). `is_blocked` is ported whole: a walker of its own height (humans 4 lifts) climbs at most one lift, drops at most one, needs headroom, and at lift 0 the terrain keeps walkers out of water and swimmers in it; the multi-tile-step variant serves big monsters. Actors use their type flags (walk, fly, swim, ethereal): npc.dat gets walk on a new game like Exult's `fix_first`, spawned monsters their monster info; saves from before these flags existed are given the same on load.
- Pathfinding is Exult's `Find_path` (`World/Pathfinder`) with `Actor_pathfinder_client`: 3D tiles, every step costs 3, lift changes and closed doors add, swamp doubles, BG cobblestone is cheaper; the open set is Exult's per-cost chains, newest first. There is no node limit: it gives up past a cost ceiling of 3× the estimate, at least three screens wide, so a walk of 200+ tiles is one search (e.g. Trinsic's east gate to 1100,2000). As in Exult, a start estimate of 512 or more (about 250 tiles) finds nothing, and the destination must be at the walker's lift.
- Walks follow Exult `Path_walking_actor_action` (`Actors/PathWalk`): one step per call at the walker's frame time; a blocked step stops the walker and is retried after 0.1-0.6 s up to 3 more times; a closed, unlocked door in the way is opened through its usecode (quality set to 0, as Exult does), walked past and closed behind (Exult's walker is stopped by then, so it hops the last tile past the door: no eggs hatch on it, as in Exult). Each door on the way opens once: Exult 1.12.1 never clears its `door_sequence_complete`, so only a walk's first door opens and a walk across a town stopped at the second (its bug, fixed, not ported; found by the new-game tester at Chantu's house, the castle's throne room and Clint's door). The tile past the door is figured from the closed door's footprint, as Exult takes it before opening (opening turns a horizontal door's shape into a vertical one; before, the walker hopped to the wrong side). Straight walks (`Zombie`) and A* walks share it. Walking frames follow Exult: the avatar cycles 1,2,1,2 and NPCs 1,0,2,0, diagonal steps use the east/west frames.
- Speeds are Exult's: milliseconds per step, with no run animation. The avatar: by the cursor's distance in a square around it (`Mouse::set_speed_cursor`) slow 400, medium 200, fast 100; in combat 266, with a hostile nearby no fast; keys fast, Shift medium (`Game/WalkSpeed`). Holding the right button, the left on open ground or a key steers a straight walk 8 tiles ahead in that direction, sidestepping a blocked direction (`start_actor_alt`); a double right-click, or a quick left click on open ground, walks an A* path to the tile (`start_actor_along_path`); letting go stops. Idle for 2 s the avatar stands. NPCs: walking to the next schedule spot 200 ms after up to 5 s (A* legs, a straight walk when no path, placed there after 2 failures or 40 legs), loiter 400 ms straight walks within 12 tiles, wander 200 ms A* legs of up to 32 tiles; party members follow at the avatar's speed (100 ms when it stands, 20 less when 5 tiles further than it from where they head), a blocked one's path three quarters of that. Formation followers step with the avatar. A held walk re-aims whenever the avatar stopped (Exult's main loop calls `start_actor` again whenever it isn't moving): a step up or down a stair stops the line walk, which was planned at the old lift, and the next is planned from the new one; before, the held walk stood on the first stair for the rest of its retries.
- Actors block like everything else (Exult counts every solid shape with height in its chunk cache; NPC and monster shapes are solid). A step onto a tile an actor stands in follows Exult `Actor::is_really_blocked`: the actor in the way steps aside for the walker (`move_aside`: to one side, else diagonally, else they swap places, `swap_positions`), unless it is walking itself, fighting, sitting, kneeling or asleep. The avatar's steering does the same when the way ahead and both sidesteps are blocked (`start_actor_alt`). Persistent walks (Exult's for the avatar and party, e.g. walking to a chair through `set_action_sequence`) plan through NPCs on their feet (`ignore_npcs`) and, blocked by one, plan again up to 30 times. A walker's own tile counts as blocked, as in Exult, so `find_spot` around an NPC finds the tiles beside it. A spot found for an actor (waking up, the party's teleport, coming on from off the screen, catching up, a clone) must also have flats the actor can stand on at lift 0: Exult's `find_spot` looks with MOVE_FLY, which lets any terrain through, so it can put a walker in water or on the solid black flat inside a cave's rock, where it never moves again (a tester's avatar, woken from a bee's sleep in the Bee Cave, 214,493; its bug, fixed, not ported, 2026-10-11). Things and monsters from eggs are placed as Exult does.
- Walking to a schedule's spot (Exult `Walk_to_schedule`, `Actors/Schedules/WalkToSchedule`) goes as far as the screen does: with the spot beyond the window enlarged by 6 tiles, a leg ends at its edge (Exult `walk_off_screen`: any tile on that line, `OnecoordPathClient`), unless the spot is near or the NPC has walked 10 legs (the avatar following it); from there, both off the screen, the NPC is put at its spot. With the NPC off the screen and the spot on it, the path is found backwards from the spot to the nearest tile off the window enlarged by 3 (`OffscreenPathClient`, aiming from where the NPC is), and the NPC is put on that tile and walks in (`PathWalk.ToTile`, Exult `from_offscreen`). A schedule change puts an NPC at its spot (if more than 12 tiles off) only when neither it nor the spot is within the activity range (Exult `teleport_offscreen_to_schedule`, for chunks not read); an NPC far off walking to a spot in range is run.

The old schedule ends before an NPC is moved to its next spot, so sleepers make their beds; a sleeper sent to sleep elsewhere gets up (Exult leaves it lying to lie down on the floor, a bug of Exult's, fixed).
- A frame's schedule ticks run over a snapshot of the NPCs and monsters: usecode run by a tick (a death, a weapon's hit, a `path_run_usecode` walk ending) that creates a monster or revives an NPC no longer breaks the loop; the newcomer acts from the next frame, as Exult adds it to the time queue.
- A saved game's schedules are restored (Exult `restore_schedule`) once the party and the combat engine are wired (`ScheduleRunner.Start`); before, an NPC saved fighting or duelling came back idle, and saved party members were not known as such.

The avatar's schedule is asked only while the avatar is in Exult's time queue (`NpcBrain.Queued`, Exult `Main_actor::handle_event`): once after an action, a `start`, the player's walk or a change of schedule, and not again until something starts it.
